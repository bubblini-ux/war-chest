# Compliance Overview

Back to [[Home]]. This is a working summary for planning, not legal advice. The customer's firearms attorney must confirm it, and rules change, so check it again before launch.

## Licences

- **Dealing in firearms** generally requires a Federal Firearms License, usually Type 01.
- **A Curio and Relic licence, Type 03, is for collecting.** It does not authorize running a dealing business. If this is all the customer holds, raise it before doing any work.
- **Dealing in machine guns and other National Firearms Act items** also requires Special Occupational Tax status, known as Class 3.

## What counts as what

- **Curio or relic:** generally a firearm at least 50 years old in its original configuration, or one on the ATF list. Most World War II firearms qualify, but the status doesn't remove other legal requirements.
- **Antique:** generally made in or before 1898. Older designs used in the war may qualify, depending on the actual date of manufacture, though most wartime production will not.
- **National Firearms Act items:** include machine guns, short-barreled rifles and shotguns, suppressors, and destructive devices. Each must be registered, and transfers need ATF approval.
- **Deactivated machine guns:** often still count as National Firearms Act items. Assume so until the registration status is confirmed.

## Red flags

Stop and send these to the attorney.

1. **An unregistered machine gun, including a family war trophy.** Registration status decides whether it can legally be sold. We never list one or help find a buyer.
2. **Live ordnance such as grenades, mortar rounds, or shells.** Treat these as dangerous. Don't move them, and call the police for bomb disposal.
3. **Any request to reactivate a deactivated weapon.** We don't publish or broker this.
4. **A buyer trying to avoid paperwork or the normal transfer process.**

## Selling across state lines

- Sales to non-licensed buyers generally ship to a licensed dealer near the buyer, who completes Form 4473 and the background check.
- Curio and relic firearms may go directly to a Type 03 holder. Verify a copy of the licence first.
- State and local law can be stricter, including bans on features, magazines, or specific models. The site has to account for this.
- Carriers have their own firearm shipping rules, so confirm them with the carrier.

## Selling abroad

Exports fall under federal export controls and the destination country's laws. Treat international sales as out of scope unless the customer already has an established and compliant process.
