# Provenance and Authentication

Back to [[Home]]. A documented history is what separates a rare piece from an old one, and it's the main thing this site is selling. Use [[Provenance Record Template]] for each item.

## Good evidence

1. Period capture or bring-back papers
2. Service records linking a veteran to the item
3. Dated photographs
4. Letters and family accounts, recorded as family accounts
5. Prior auction records and sale receipts
6. Written opinions from recognized specialists

Bring-back papers don't make an unregistered machine gun legal to sell. See [[Compliance Overview]].

## Authenticity checks

The customer or an outside specialist makes the judgment. We only present the result.

1. Do the markings fit the maker and date?
2. Do serial numbers match where they should?
3. Is the finish original or refinished?
4. Is there an import mark showing later commercial import?
5. Are any parts replaced or from another period?

## Words we use

- **Documented:** paperwork directly supports the claim.
- **Attributed:** evidence points to it, but doesn't prove it.
- **Family history:** passed down and not independently verified.
- **Unknown:** we say so.

## Fakes and overstatement

Reproductions and fake markings exist, and some paperwork gets forged. A false claim damages the customer's reputation and may bring legal liability, so the site never goes further than the evidence.
